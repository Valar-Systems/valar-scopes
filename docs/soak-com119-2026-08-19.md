### And the same-day control that settles it outright

COM119 was reflashed at 17:21 for the factory-reset acceptance runs. A **fresh
boot, fresh heap, same logbook** (`[logbook] loaded 258 types (27 claimed) ...
13137 contacts`):

```
[health] ... heap free=43148 largest=31732 ... rej=0 allocFail=0
[fetch]  LOW HEAP after Blipscope Cloud: free=33812 largest=16372 aircraft=40
```

**Seconds apart.** `largest` goes 31,732 -> 16,372 on the first cloud fetch that
returns 40 aircraft, on a heap that is minutes old and has fragmented nothing.

That removes the last reading in which time or accumulated fragmentation is the
cause. The tracked-aircraft working set alone accounts for the whole collapse,
and a 46-hour soak was never needed to produce it -- one busy fetch does it.

## The two thresholds, which are not the same number

- **16,717 B** — `heaphealth::TLS_HANDSHAKE_BYTES`, the measured size of a real
  mbedTLS handshake block. This is the **functional cliff**: below it,
  `CanHandshake()` fails and enrichment is refused.
- **20,000 B** — `LARGEST_BLOCK_BUDGET` in the health line. A **soak warning**,
  not a gate. Nothing behaves differently at 20,000.

`largest` oscillates in 1,024 B steps between **16,372 and 19,444**, and
**16,372 < 16,717 < 17,396**. The device straddles the TLS cliff by **345 bytes**.
On a sample that lands at 16,372 every handshake trial fails; one step up and
they all succeed. That is why `rej` climbs in bursts and why `tlsOk` flips
between samples rather than latching — and it is why "BUDGET BROKEN" at 07:38 and
"enrichment actually dead" at ~09:00 are **different events an hour apart**.

## FINDING: heap fragmentation under TRAFFIC LOAD starves enrichment

*(Named for the variable the evidence supports. The working title was "under
logbook growth"; this capture does not support that and COM118 contradicts it.)*

**Severity: launch-relevant.** Not a bench curiosity.

- **Invisible to the customer.** Frames stay at 41–43 ms, every fetch returns
  `ok=1 http=200`, the radar is completely normal. The screen looks perfect.
- **Permanently kills the paid-for content.** Types, operators and photos stop
  resolving for as long as the sky is busy. A customer in a dense airspace gets
  *fewer* enriched cards than one in an empty one — the opposite of the product
  promise, and it degrades exactly when there is most to look at.
- **Self-inflicted amplification.** At 23 rejections/s the device is trial-
  allocating ~16.7 KB and freeing it 23 times a second, forever, achieving
  nothing. That is not free.

Related: [`logbook-store-growth-notes.md`](logbook-store-growth-notes.md) — the
logbook's NVS growth is a real and separate concern; this finding is **not** an
instance of it, and the two should not be merged.

### Proposed fixes — not built, in rough order of confidence

1. **Cap the enrichment retry rate.** A rejected trial should back off, not
   re-fire on the next loop. 23/s achieving nothing is pure waste and it is the
   cheapest thing to fix. Does not solve the starvation; stops it burning CPU.
2. **Bound the tracked working set by memory, not only by count.**
   `MAX_AIRCRAFT` is 60 and 40 was enough to starve the heap. The cut should
   consider available contiguous heap, not just a count that was chosen before
   the cloud feed existed.
3. **Shrink per-aircraft residency.** Each `TrackedAircraft` holds several
   `String`s; 40 of them churning every 15 s is a fragmentation engine. Fixed
   buffers for the short fields (ICAO, type code, squawk) would cut both the
   churn and the floor.
4. **Reserve the handshake block.** Allocate 16,717 B once at boot and keep it
   for TLS. Turns an intermittent starvation into a fixed, known cost.
   Trade-off: takes the memory from the tracked set permanently.
5. **Serve enrichment from the Worker in the feed response.** Removes the
   device-side handshake from the hot path entirely. Largest change, and it
   makes the device's heap irrelevant to whether cards fill in.

### CORRECTION 2026-08-19, and the PASS is narrower than it first read

**These numbers prove the chip ANSWERS ON I2C. They do not prove it reports a
finger, and it does not.**

Later the same day, with a human deliberately tapping the panel for 14 minutes,
COM119 logged **zero `[touch]` events** — on a build with `SOAK_TEST` compiled
out, so nothing was intercepting samples. COM118 logged 49 in the same window.
COM119's 46-hour soak also contains zero.

So "0 wedges, 0 recoveries, 0 ms outage" is a true statement about the
watchdog's own synthetic probes and a **misleading one about touch**. The
absence of touch events in this capture was recorded above as "the A/B never
ran"; the honest reading is that it may not have been RUNNABLE, and that
question was not asked at the time.

The touch verdict is therefore **NOT PASS — UNDETERMINED, pending station 1**,
whose purpose changes accordingly: the question is no longer "can this panel
sustain a 10 s contact" but "does this panel report contact at all". See
[`bench-runbook-device-game.md`](bench-runbook-device-game.md).

## The static-hold A/B NEVER RAN

**Zero `[touch]` events in the entire capture.** `touchIdle` runs 131,735 s →
165,324 s without resetting — the panel was not touched once in 9 h 20 m, and had
not been for 36.6 h before that.

The 16,614 probes are **TouchWatchdog's own synthetic probes**, which by design
run only at moments the chip is provably awake. They say nothing about a held
finger.

**This soak answers nothing about static touches.** The §13 B question — can this
panel sustain a 10 s contact — remains open and still requires a human finger per
the harness protocol (station 1, [`bench-runbook-device-game.md`](bench-runbook-device-game.md)).
It stays on the docket.
